Strong gravitational lenses are rare in Euclid Q1, about one per 2200 objects searched, and too few are catalogued to train a finder on. We train finders on simulated arcs painted onto real Euclid galaxies and measure how much a training signal from real, unlabelled images improves them at a search's operating point: the true-positive rate (TPR) at a false-positive rate of 10^-3. No real-lens label is a training target, but in our main design labels shape the target: it is built from catalogued lenses and random galaxies, and every target batch is balanced 1:1 with the catalogue labels. Against a control identical to our DANN arm except that its adaptation weight is zero, DANN raises TPR from 0.18 to 0.50, a gain of +0.31 [+0.06, +0.57], in a contrast registered before the control ran but after the adaptation arms' results were known, on a test pool from which we removed catalogued lenses and simulation hosts after pre-registration; CORAL, FreeMatch and BNM reach 0.40 to 0.52, and each adaptation arm's contrast against the control, which is matched to DANN alone, excludes zero. In a second pre-registered experiment, a first test toward survey prevalence, we diluted the target to one lens per ten galaxies and drew its batches without the labels. DANN still raises TPR over the control, by +0.33 [+0.29, +0.38], and BNM by +0.19 [+0.003, +0.39], though for both the comparison with the balanced target cannot rule out that much of the original gain was lost; for CORAL and FreeMatch we cannot detect a gain. Dilution also cuts the number of lenses per batch and how often each is seen, so it does not isolate the class proportion, and the diluted pool is still built from the catalogue. At about 98 test lenses per fold, no single-checkpoint comparison separates CORAL, DANN and FreeMatch. Our simulation-only baseline is far below a published one, so all gains are measured from a low starting point.
